# The challenge

Many communities still rebuild reactively: funding follows the damage report
instead of the resilience plan.

- Resources arrive after the event
- Plans sit on shelves, disconnected from budgets
- Grant reporting rarely feeds back into the next plan

Note:
Frame this as the status quo most officials already recognise. The goal is not
to blame any office but to name the pattern we intend to change.

# Three pillars for recovery

::callout
Resilience is a budget decision, not a weather forecast.
::

Each pillar carries its own funding line and its own accountability measure.

- Prevention — shared risk mapping across departments
- Response — pre-approved spending triggers for declared events
- Recovery — multi-year rebuilding windows instead of one-off grants

Note:
Explain that the callout is the thesis of the whole deck: we can plan for
disruption before it happens, and that planning has to be funded.

Script:
Lead with the callout, then walk each pillar as one sentence each. Save the
detail for the data slide.

::table

| Prevention | $120M | $140M | $160M |
| Response | $90M | $95M | $95M |
| Recovery | $60M | $80M | $100M |
| Total | $270M | $315M | $355M |
::

Prevention grows fastest, and response funding floors decline as prevention
matures. All figures are annual allocations to regional partners.

Note:
Read the anti-diagonal: as prevention rises, the recovery burden is expected to
fall. Flag that 2028 response funding is the planned floor, not the actual spend.

# Next steps

This framework lands in three moves.

1. Adopt the shared risk map template in your region this quarter
2. Align one budget line with a pre-approved trigger
3. Report outcomes against the recovery cost baseline by next spring
